ZeroDiff++: Balancing Semantic Diffusion Dynamics for Robust Zero-Shot
Learning
Qin Li Qi Li Limei Liu Junfeng Yang Han Peng ∗
Hunan University of Technology and Business, China
Abstract
Zero-shot learning (ZSL) aims to recognize unseen
classes by leveraging shared semantic embeddings. Re-
cent diffusion-based approaches, such as ZeroDiff, have
achieved promising results by improving visual-semantic
alignment via denoising-based generation and supervised
contrastive learning. However, they adopt a fixed diffusion
schedule for all classes, ignoring differences in semantic
complexity. Simple classes tend to be over-diffused, los-
ing discriminative cues, whereas complex classes remain
under-diffused with insufficient semantic disentanglement,
leading to a structural mismatch that we term Semantic Dif-
fusion Imbalance (SDI). To mitigate SDI, we propose Ze-
roDiff++, a three-path diffusion framework designed to bal-
ance semantic dynamics across classes of different complex-
ity. In addition to the original ZeroDiff pathway, ZeroD-
iff++ introduces: (i) a weak diffusion branch (W-Branch)
to prevent excessive denoising in simple classes, and (ii)
a mild diffusion branch (M-Branch) with smoother noise
scheduling that enables complex classes to more reliably
preserve their semantic patterns. Together, these branches
maintain semantic balance during denoising, yielding more
robust visual-semantic alignment. Extensive experiments
on standard ZSL benchmarks demonstrate that ZeroDiff++
achieves state-of-the-art performance, validating the im-
portance of addressing SDI in diffusion-based ZSL.
1. Introduction
Zero-shot learning (ZSL) seeks to classify unseen classes by
transferring knowledge from seen ones via shared seman-
tic embeddings [1, 26, 41]. Recently, diffusion-based mod-
els such as ZeroDiff [47] have advanced this field by intro-
ducing a denoising diffusion process that solidifies visual-
semantic correlation through generative regularization and
supervised contrastive learning.
While diffusion-based models strengthen vi-
sual–semantic correlation under limited data, they still

schedule to promote semantic correlation stability. These
two branches collaborate with the primary path to restore
semantic equilibrium during denoising, yielding more bal-
anced and robust feature generation under limited supervi-
sion. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• To the best of our knowledge, SDI has not been explic-

2. Related Work
2.1. Zero-Shot Learning
The fundamental idea of ZSL is to align seen and unseen
classes via auxiliary semantic information. Existing ZSL
research falls into two main categories: embedding-based
and generative methods.
Embedding-based ZSL.Embedding-based methods learn
a mapping between visual and semantic spaces [12, 16, 34,
39, 44–46, 52], like Attribute Prototype Network [44] and
Semantic Contrastive Embedding [16]. In addition, several
studies have demonstrated that region-level features offer
stronger discriminative cues than holistic representations [4,
5, 7, 21, 49]. However, the above methods are trained solely
on seen classes, and tend to overfit the seen domain and
degrade significantly when training data are limited [4, 46].
Generative ZSL.Generative approaches synthesize unseen
visual features via generative models, thus converting ZSL
into a supervised learning problem [2, 3, 9, 20, 30, 42].
This line has achieved strong performance, particularly with
large diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion [32], which
can serve as zero-shot classifiers without retraining [27].
Despite this progress, generative ZSL remains constrained
by limited data for modeling high-dimensional distributions
and by reliance on strong semantic conditioning, which to-
gether limit generalization and lead to unstable feature gen-
eration. A number of works aim to improve diversity and
stability of generative models under restricted data. For in-
stance, hybrid V AE–GAN frameworks [28, 35, 42] attempt
to mitigate mode collapse, yet their performance remains
sensitive to reduced training data. Diffusion-based gener-
ative models [14, 17] provide a more stable alternative by
learning data distributions through progressive denoising.
Among these efforts, ZeroDiff [47] represents a partic-
ularly promising direction. By revisiting supervised con-
trastive learning [25] and leveraging high intra-class vari-
ance [22] as fine-grained semantic cues, ZeroDiff inte-
grates a denoising diffusion with semantic discrimination,
enabling visual-semantic alignment even under limited su-
pervision. However, ZeroDiff relies on a single and fixed
reverse diffusion schedule for all classes, ignoring the fact
that semantic concepts differ substantially in complexity.
This gives rise to uneven denoising behavior across classes,
resulting in imbalanced semantic reconstruction.
2.2. Generative Models
Constructing generative models to synthesize additional
samples has become a reliable strategy for data augmenta-
tion in low-data regimes [23, 51]. However, training effec-
tive generative models typically requires abundant labeled
data, which makes them difficult to deploy in data-scarce
settings [33, 37]. To alleviate this constraint, recent studies
have explored data-efficient generative modeling from both
training and model-design perspectives. From the training
side, DiffAugment [50] introduces differentiable augmen-
tation to stabilize training, while AdvAug [8] explores the
lottery ticket hypothesis to improve data efficiency. From
the model-design side, PatchDiff [36] adopts patch-wise
coordinate-conditioned score matching for efficient diffu-
sion learning, and DDGAN [43] reformulates diffusion with
a multimodal conditional GAN to enable faster sampling.
Large-scale text-to-image diffusion models, such as Sta-
ble Diffusion [32] and DALL-E2 [31], reveal the poten-
tial of guided imagination for scalable data synthesis. Al-
though these models can generate labeled samples without
human annotation, they are trained on vast open-domain
datasets that naturally include instances of unseen classes.

3. Preliminaries
3.1. Problem Setting
LetY s andY u denote the label sets of seen and unseen
classes, whereY s ∩Y u =∅. The training dataset is defined
as:D tr ={(x s, ys,a s)|x s ∈ X s, ys ∈ Y s,a s ∈ A s},
wherex s denotes a visual sample,y s is its corresponding
class label, anda s represents the semantic embedding (e.g.,
attribute or text feature) associated with classy s. Simi-
larly, the unseen-class dataset is represented as:D te =
{(xu, yu,a u)|x u ∈ X u, yu ∈ Y u,a u ∈ A u}, where a vi-
sual samplex u belongs to an unseen class. The goal of ZSL
is to useD tr to learn a mappingf zsl :X u → Y u, which
correctly assigns unseen samples to their classes. In the
generalized ZSL (GZSL) setting, a unified mappingf gzsl
needs to be learned based onD tr:X s ∪ X u → Y s ∪ Y u.

4.2. ZeroDiff++
To address SDI, we propose ZeroDiff++, an enhanced diffu-
sion framework that rethinks the denoising dynamics from
a semantic equilibrium perspective. Instead of one diffu-
sion trajectory, ZeroDiff++ decomposes the generative pro-
cess into three coordinated paths: a primary path preserv-
ing the ZeroDiff behavior, and two complementary auxil-
iary branches, W-Branch and M-Branch, to restore seman-
tic equilibrium during denoising. We first introduce core
innovations of ZeroDiff++ and then describe the overview.

4.2.2. Overview
During training, ZeroDiff++ augments the original ZeroDiff
with two auxiliary W- and M-branches and the SGDF mod-
ule. The W-branch employs weak diffusion to enhance fea-
ture discriminability, whereas the M-branch adopts a mild
schedule to stabilize semantic evolution. SGDF further re-
fines denoised features at each step and improves seman-
tic–visual alignment. Finally, two generatorsR ∗ andG ∗
can be obtained. We summarize the training pipeline of Ze-
roDiff++ in Algorithm 1. At reference time, ZeroDiff++
employsR ∗ andG ∗ to synthesize pseudo representations
and features for unseen classes and subsequently trains a
lightweight classifier for zero-shot recognition.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we identify SDI as a previously overlooked
limitation of diffusion-based ZSL, where a fixed noise
schedule leads to uneven semantic preservation across
classes. To address this issue, we propose ZeroDiff++, a
three-path diffusion framework, to explicitly balance se-
mantic dynamics. By integrating the W-Branch to avoid
over-denoising and the M-Branch to enhance semantic re-
coverability, ZeroDiff++ achieves stable and discriminative
visual–semantic alignment. Extensive experiments across
multiple ZSL/GZSL benchmarks demonstrate that ZeroD-
iff++ consistently outperforms existing SOTA methods.
